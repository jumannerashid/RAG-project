The objective of this study was to assess the usage of credit scoring in
the process of credit risk management in commercial banks in Tanzania.
The study was based on the dimensions of how credit scoring facilitates
the evaluation of borrowers in making lending decisions at the time of
loan application to ensure the asymmetrical information problem is
avoided hence a means to an effective credit risk management for
commercial banks in Tanzania. The study was assessed on the three areas
namely; the process of capturing borrowers’ information, how the credit
scoring system generates the scorecard reports and usefulness of the
scorecards generated in credit risk management. A case study design was
deployed in conducting the study while the approaches used in the study
were simple random and purposive sampling. The Population was composed
of bank staff from credit section at Advans who are the main users of
the credit scoring reports on a daily basis. Data collection was done
using questionnaires and documentary instruments while the analysis was
done using SPSS. Findings suggests that, the information gap between
lenders and borrowers intensifies the credit risk in the lending
business, hence more than one factor need to be considered to ensure the
proper evaluation of borrowers is performed to make good lending
decisions. Moreover, credit-scoring gives a good insight on the
borrowers as a starting point hence the credit analysts need to go
further on their evaluations before the final lending decisions. The
study concludes that, the use of credit scoring to evaluate borrowers
helps in building the asymmetrical information gap which acts as a major
catalyst for credit risk and hence, credit analysts are encouraged to
consider the tool before making the lending decisions for the commercial
banks in order to manage the risk.
